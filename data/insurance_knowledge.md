# Insurance Mitra: an educational guide to insurance

This guide provides general education, not a contract, legal advice, or a statement of current
regulatory requirements. Insurance terms vary by product, insurer, jurisdiction, and the wording
of each policy. Check the complete policy schedule, wording, endorsements, and current applicable
rules. Examples below are educational and are not promises of cover or claim payment.

## What insurance does

Insurance is an agreement under which an insurer may provide specified benefits when defined
events occur, subject to the contract's conditions, limits, exclusions, and applicable law. A
policy is not a guarantee that every loss will be paid. The proposal, schedule, wording, and
endorsements should be read together. Ask the insurer or a qualified adviser to clarify
uncertainties before relying on a benefit.

## Life, health, and motor insurance

Life insurance provides a benefit described in the contract on specified events, which may include
death or survival to a stated date depending on product design. A sum assured is a stated benefit
amount in many life products; it is not necessarily the amount payable in every circumstance.
Terms, exclusions, disclosures, and product structure matter.

Health insurance may cover defined medical expenses or provide stated benefits, subject to the
policy's scope, limits, waiting periods, exclusions, network arrangements, and claim process.
Hospitalization alone does not establish that an expense is payable.

Motor insurance relates to specified risks involving a vehicle. Policy types and legally required
cover can differ by jurisdiction and change over time. Do not infer the scope of own-damage,
third-party, driver, passenger, or add-on protection without checking current local requirements
and the particular policy schedule and wording.

## Premiums, policy periods, and renewal

A premium is the price charged for the insurance contract or period of cover. Its calculation and
payment schedule depend on the product and insurer; this guide does not state current market
prices. A policy period is the start and end period recorded in the schedule. Confirm the effective
dates, payment status, renewal deadline, grace-period terms if any, and consequences of a lapse
directly in the contract and with the insurer.

Renewal offers may have conditions and need not be identical to the prior term. Review changes in
benefits, limits, price, exclusions, and endorsements before renewing. Cancellation rights,
refunds, notice, and effects vary; consult the applicable policy wording and rules rather than
assuming a universal cancellation process.

## Sum insured, sum assured, deductibles, and co-payments

Sum insured commonly describes a maximum or stated insurance amount for certain general or health
benefits, subject to the policy's limits and terms. Sum assured commonly describes a stated benefit
in life insurance. Usage varies, so the policy definitions control.

A deductible is an amount or threshold that the insured may have to bear before specified cover
responds. A co-payment is a specified share of an eligible expense borne by the insured. They are
not interchangeable, and their application, calculation, and interaction with limits depend on
the wording. Educational example: with an eligible expense of 10,000 and a 10% co-payment, the
insured share might be 1,000 if the contract applies that percentage to the full expense; actual
calculation can differ and the example is not contractual.

## Waiting periods and exclusions

A waiting period is a stated time during which specified benefits or conditions may not be
available, subject to exact policy terms and any applicable rules. Policies may describe different
periods for different benefits or circumstances. Confirm when each period starts and what it
applies to.

An exclusion identifies a loss, condition, event, or circumstance that is not covered or is
limited, as defined in the wording. Search the policy for headings such as exclusions, general
exclusions, limitations, and non-payable items, but read linked definitions and exceptions too.
Do not treat a summary or search result as a substitute for the full clause.

## Common claim steps and documents

The correct process and required documents depend on the policy, event, and insurer. Common
educational steps are: notify the insurer or claims administrator promptly using the stated
channel; record the claim or reference number; follow instructions; retain originals and copies;
and provide accurate information. Do not delay urgent medical care or emergency response to
complete insurance paperwork.

Documents that may be requested can include a completed claim form, policy details, identity or
contact information, bills and receipts, medical records or discharge summaries for a health
claim, and an incident or loss report for a motor claim. These are examples only, not a guaranteed
or exhaustive checklist. Ask the insurer for the requirements for the specific claim and check
the policy's notification deadlines.

## Cashless and reimbursement claims

In a cashless arrangement, a network provider may coordinate eligible expenses with the insurer
or administrator, subject to authorization and policy terms. Cashless does not mean every bill or
service is automatically payable; non-covered amounts may remain with the policyholder.

For reimbursement, the policyholder generally pays first and submits a claim for review. The
insurer assesses it against the policy and submitted evidence. Timelines, forms, eligible expenses,
and required records vary. Confirm the process with the insurer before assuming either route is
available.

## Delays and disputed claims

Possible reasons for delay or dispute include missing or inconsistent information, incomplete
supporting records, late notification, questions about eligibility or policy status, a limit or
exclusion, or a need for further assessment. These are general possibilities, not a conclusion
about any claim. Request the reason and relevant policy clause in writing, submit requested
information through official channels, keep dated copies, and use the complaint or review route
stated by the insurer and applicable rules. No particular outcome is guaranteed.

## How to read a policy document

Start with the schedule: identify the insured person or property, policy number, period, selected
benefits, limits, premium status, and endorsements. Then read definitions, coverage grants,
conditions, exclusions, claim notification and documentation clauses, cancellation and renewal
terms, and grievance information. Check that the schedule and wording agree. Searchable text is
helpful, but a scan, table, footnote, endorsement, or cross-reference can be missed by software.
Ask the insurer to explain ambiguous or conflicting clauses and retain the response.

## Fraud prevention and safe document handling

Use verified insurer contact details and official payment channels. Be cautious of unsolicited
messages, guaranteed claim or policy promises, requests for one-time passwords, remote-access
software, or payment to personal accounts. Verify a representative independently; never share
passwords or one-time authentication codes. Report suspected fraud through official channels.

Policy PDFs can contain names, addresses, identity numbers, medical history, vehicle details, and
financial information. Upload only if necessary and only to a service you trust. Remove unrelated
pages and redact sensitive identifiers where feasible before sharing. Do not upload passwords or
authentication codes. In Insurance Mitra, uploaded PDFs are processed for the current Streamlit
session and held in session memory; they are not added to the shared persisted index or
intentionally written to disk. The server operator and hosting provider may still control the
runtime environment, so this is not a promise of confidential or regulated data processing.
Remove documents from the sidebar or end the session to discard the app's in-session copy.
